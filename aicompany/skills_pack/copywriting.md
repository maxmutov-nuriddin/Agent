1. Gather context. Ask at most 3 short questions, only for what is missing; otherwise assume and list the assumptions at the end.
   - ONE action the reader should take (call, order, visit, subscribe).
   - Audience: who, their problem, objections, and the words THEY use.
   - Offer: what is sold, what is different from alternatives, the real outcome, proof (numbers, reviews, years in business).
   - Channel: Instagram, Telegram, website, flyer, ad. It sets the length and tone.
2. Principles.
   - Clear beats clever. If the reader must decode a line, rewrite it.
   - Benefits over features: say what it means for the customer.
   - Specific beats vague: "Zapchastni 10 daqiqada topamiz" instead of "Tez xizmat".
   - Customer language over company language. One idea per section.
   - Honest: never invent numbers, reviews or guarantees. Mark missing proof as [KERAK: isbot] and ask for it.
3. Style. Simple words, active voice, confident (cut "juda", "ancha", "really"), no exclamation spam, no buzzwords without a fact behind them. If a line would fit a competitor unchanged, rewrite it with your real differentiator (or flag [KERAK: farq]).
4. No AI tells (readers reject copy that sounds generated). Never write:
   - contrast reveals: "It's not X, it's Y", "Nafaqat X, balki Y". State Y with the reason;
   - negation lists: "No setup, no fees, no waiting". Say what happens; one real absence ("Karta shart emas") near the CTA is fine;
   - a claim followed by trailing clauses: end the sentence at the claim;
   - self-answered questions: "Natija? 3 barobar tez." Just say it;
   - stock openers: "In today's fast world", "Unlock the power of", "Take it to the next level", "Say goodbye to";
   - em dashes in short copy. At most one fragment and one list of three per section.
5. Page structure. Headline (the single most important message; formulas: "{Natija} {og'riq nuqtasisiz}", "{Auditoriya} uchun {kategoriya}", "Endi hech qachon {yoqimsiz hodisa}"); subheadline (1-2 sentences, adds specifics); primary CTA; proof; problem; 3-5 benefits; how it works in 3 steps; objections/FAQ and guarantee; final CTA that repeats the value.
6. CTA = action verb + what they get. Weak: "Yuborish", "Batafsil", "Boshlash". Strong: "Narxni bilish", "Bepul maslahat olish", "Buyurtma berish", "Ro'yxatni yuklab olish".
7. Voice. Pick formality and personality first (friendly-professional by default for Uzbek small business); headlines may be bolder, body copy clearer, CTAs most direct. Write in the owner's language (Uzbek by default, Latin script) unless asked otherwise.
8. Self-check before delivering: every claim is backed by a fact the owner gave; every sentence over 20 words is shortened; no banned patterns above; read it aloud.
9. Output: the copy by section; 2-3 options for headline and CTA, each with a one-line rationale; a short list of assumptions and [KERAK: ...] gaps; page title and meta description if it is for the web.
